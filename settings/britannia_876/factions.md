# Factions — Britannia 876 Setting Pack

## The West Saxon Court
Alfred's own household - ealdormen, thegns, and household warriors of Wessex. Loyal by default
(`hall_loyalty` starts high) but not infinitely patient; repeated defeat or a visibly failing king
should erode it like anything else. The most immediate lever the player has for `military_readiness`
and `treasury`.

## The Church in Wessex
Bishops and abbots, led in spirit by figures like Wærferth of Worcester. A Christian king's
legitimacy runs substantially through the Church's backing - `church_favor` is not a soft or purely
flavor resource, it's load-bearing for `witan_authority` too. The Church also has its own interests
(land, protection from raiding, patronage of learning) that don't always perfectly align with purely
military priorities.

## Mercia
A fellow English kingdom under its own severe Viking pressure, currently ruled by Ceolwulf II under
circumstances the Chronicle later frames unkindly. Whether Mercia becomes a real partner or stays a
wary, self-interested neighbor is largely a function of `saxon_unity` and `foreign_standing` - and
of the player's choices toward Ceolwulf specifically.

## Guthrum's Host
The immediate military threat - a large, well-organized Viking force with its own internal politics
(alliances among various Danish leaders, tribute arrangements, competing objectives) that the GM
should feel free to play as more than a monolith when it serves the story.

## The Wider Danelaw
Halfdan Ragnarsson's power in the North and the broader Great Army network beyond Guthrum's
immediate force - mostly an offstage pressure at campaign start, but a real one, and a plausible
source of later escalation or, in a heavily diverged campaign, negotiation.

## The Franks
Alfred's court had real, if distant, contact with the Carolingian world. Mostly relevant to
`foreign_standing` and as a hedge - a place potential exile, marriage alliances, or plain moral
support could plausibly come from if Wessex's position worsens.
