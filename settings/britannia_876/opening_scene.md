The rain has not stopped in three days, and the hall at Wilton smells of wet wool and tallow smoke.
You are Alfred, King of the West Saxons, and the news from the west is bad in the specific,
grinding way it has been bad for years now: Wareham fallen, then Exeter taken after Guthrum's ships
slipped past your fleet in the storm that should have finished him. Tribute bought you time before.
It may not buy you time again.

Ealhswith sits near the fire, quiet, watching you the way she watches you when she already knows
what you're thinking before you've said it aloud. Somewhere beyond the hall doors, ealdormen who
swore oaths to your brothers before they swore them to you are waiting to hear what the King of
Wessex intends to do about the Danes camped in the heart of his kingdom.

You have a kingdom that is not yet lost, a treasury thinner than it should be, a church that trusts
you more than your own nobles sometimes do, and a long, cold winter ahead of you either way.

What do you do first?
