The messenger is still dripping when they bring him in. He has ridden from the west in two days on
three horses, and he says it standing, because nobody has told him he may sit: Guthrum is inside
the walls of Exeter. The ships that should have held him at Wareham were scattered by the storm.
Enough of his men got through.

Nobody in the hall at Wilton speaks. Ealhswith sets her cup down without a sound.

Odda of Devon moves first. He is a big man going grey, and he has to step over a hound to reach
you. "Lord. Exeter is my shire. Give me the fyrd of Devon and Somerset and I'll sit outside his gate
until he eats his horses."

From the bench by the door, Wulfhere of Wiltshire laughs without much humor. "And pay for it how?
We paid him at Wareham. We'll pay him again. It's the one thing a Dane understands."

Bishop Waerferth says nothing at all, which from him is a kind of opinion.

The rain keeps on against the shutters. Every man in the room is looking at you.

Scene zero, November 876. The council is waiting on Alfred's answer: march on Exeter (Odda), pay
again (Wulfhere), or something else. Wulfhere is the setting's eventual deserter - play him as
reasonable, not obviously treacherous. Let the player's first move set the tone; nothing here
needs a check.
