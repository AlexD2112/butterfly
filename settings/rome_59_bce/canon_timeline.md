# Canon Timeline — Rome 59 BCE Setting Pack

Locked events have already happened as the campaign opens. Contingent events are the real
historical path the campaign can follow toward or diverge from - firing one should feel earned
through play via `canon_event_triggered`, not assigned on a schedule.

## Locked (already true at campaign start)
- Caesar is Consul, with Bibulus as his openly obstructive colleague.
- The First Triumvirate (Caesar, Pompey, Crassus) is an established, if still officially informal,
  fact of Roman politics.
- Caesar has been Pontifex Maximus since 63 BCE.
- Clodius has recently been transferred to plebeian status with Caesar's help, clearing his path to
  stand for tribune.

## Contingent (the historical path, not guaranteed)
- **Lex Julia agraria passed** - Caesar's land redistribution law for veterans and the urban poor,
  pushed through over fierce optimate resistance and Bibulus's obstruction.
- **Julia marries Pompey** - the marriage sealing the Triumvirate's alliance.
- **Vatinian law grants Gaul** - the law granting Caesar an extended proconsular command over
  Cisalpine Gaul and Illyricum (with Transalpine Gaul added soon after) - the seed of everything
  that follows.
- **Clodius engineers Cicero's exile** - historically in 58 BCE, following Clodius's law targeting
  anyone who executed a citizen without trial (aimed squarely at Cicero over the Catiline
  conspirators). Whether this fires, and whether the player intervenes, is a real fork.
- **Caesar departs for Gaul** - the start of the Gallic campaigns.
- **Conference of Luca** (56 BCE) - the Triumvirate's renewal and reinforcement, years down the
  line - included here as a long-horizon contingent event for a campaign that runs that far.
- **Crossing of the Rubicon** (49 BCE) - the furthest-horizon contingent event in this pack, marking
  the Triumvirate's final collapse into civil war - not expected to fire in a short campaign, but
  present for a long one.

## Divergence guidance
This setting has an unusually large gap between "what's locked" (very little - the campaign opens
mid-crisis, with almost everything ahead of it still contingent) and "what's famous" (almost
everything that makes 59 BCE historically pivotal hasn't happened yet at campaign start). The GM
should treat the Gaul command, Cicero's fate, and the Triumvirate's durability as genuinely open,
not scripted certainties - a campaign where Caesar never goes to Gaul, or goes on different terms,
is a legitimate and interesting divergence, not a failure to hit history's marks.
