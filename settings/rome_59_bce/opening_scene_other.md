Someone has painted it on the wall of the fullers' shop on the Argiletum, in letters a foot high:
the year of the consuls JULIUS and CAESAR. The joke has been all over the city since Bibulus locked
himself in his house and started declaring the gods against every vote his colleague takes.

The Forum is full by the second hour. Pompey's veterans are here for the land bill, the moneylenders
are here because the veterans are, and the pickpockets are here for all of them.

A man in a very good toga falls into step beside you on the Sacra Via. You have never seen him
before. He smiles as though you have.

"You were at Clodius's house yesterday," he says pleasantly. "Marcus Tullius would like a word with
you. Today, if you can manage it. Before you go back there."
