# Canon Timeline — Crusades 1097 Setting Pack

Locked events have already happened as the campaign opens (June 1097). Contingent events are the
real historical path the campaign can follow toward or diverge from - firing one should feel earned
through play via `canon_event_triggered`, not assigned on a schedule.

## Locked (already true at campaign start)
- **Nicaea falls** to the crusaders after a siege, but is surrendered to Byzantine forces rather
  than sacked by the crusaders themselves - a real point of resentment among the rank and file, who
  expected plunder.
- All major crusader leaders present have sworn (or are understood to have sworn) an oath of
  fealty to Alexios I regarding reclaimed former Byzantine territory - see the `oath_to_alexios`
  commitment.

## Contingent (the historical path, not guaranteed)
- **Battle of Dorylaeum** - a hard-fought crusader victory over Seljuk forces under Kilij Arslan,
  shortly after the campaign opens.
- **Siege of Antioch begins** - the long, grueling siege that defines the middle of the First
  Crusade's march.
- **Antioch falls to the crusaders** - historically through a betrayal from within the city's
  defenses, not a direct assault.
- **Kerbogha's relief army defeated** - a Seljuk relief force arrives just after Antioch falls and
  is defeated in turn, one of the crusade's most dramatic near-run outcomes.
- **Bohemond claims Antioch** - Bohemond declares himself Prince of Antioch, in practice reneging
  on the oath to Alexios - the central Bohemond-specific historical beat this setting is built
  around, and very much a live question rather than a certainty.
- **Siege of Jerusalem** and **Jerusalem falls** - the crusade's ultimate objective, well beyond
  the Antioch arc and included here mainly for campaigns that run that long.

## Divergence guidance
Bohemond's actual, real-world choice to break his oath and keep Antioch for himself is the
single most obvious divergence point this setting is built to explore - but it isn't the only one.
The GM should treat the crusader leaders' council, the Byzantine relationship, and even whether
Antioch falls the way it historically did as genuinely open, logging specific divergences rather
than assuming the "famous" outcome is the default one.
